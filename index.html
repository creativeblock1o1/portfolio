PĀṆINI

Sovereignty is often understood through the language of territory, borders and the authority of the State. Yet, in an increasingly interconnected and contested world, its exercise extends far beyond the geographical boundaries of a nation. It is reflected in the strength of institutions, the integrity of its civic foundations, the resilience of its society and its capacity to safeguard national interests against forces that operate across physical, political and informational domains.

It is this broader understanding of sovereignty that forms the foundation of Pāṇini, Adhyāya III.

Read more

Pāṇini is a research initiative by TIES, conceived to examine consequential questions of national importance through structured inquiry, research and informed perspectives. With this edition, we take Pāṇini into a magazine-style format, bringing together multiple contributors under a common editorial theme. The intention is to give connected strategic questions the depth, continuity and space they deserve.

Titled Mapping the Fault Lines of Sovereignty, this Adhyāya examines five dimensions of the security challenges confronting Bharat.

We begin with the Indian state's campaign against Left-Wing Extremism, examining the strategic effort to restore its authority across territories that remained affected by insurgency for decades, alongside the questions that will shape the post-insurgency future. We then turn to the Khalistan threat and the transnational networks that seek to sustain separatist activity beyond India's borders. The third विमर्श examines infiltration, demographic change, citizenship and the integrity of the Republic's civic foundations.

The inquiry subsequently moves into the cognitive domain, where narratives, information networks and psychological influence have acquired an increasingly consequential place in modern conflict. Finally, we turn to India's eastern frontier, tracing the intersection of narcotics, insurgency, illicit economies and regional geopolitics across the India-Myanmar borderlands.

These are distinct subjects, yet each raises a fundamental question: how does a nation preserve its sovereignty when the pressures upon it are increasingly dispersed, interconnected and difficult to contain?

The answers require more than an examination of security operations or policy announcements. They demand an understanding of institutions, strategic intent, historical circumstances, political realities and the people whose lives are directly affected by these developments. National security must therefore be examined alongside the responsibilities of governance, constitutional order and the long-term interests of the Republic.

As you turn these pages, I invite you to approach each विमर्श as part of a larger inquiry into the changing nature of sovereignty. The objective is to encourage informed engagement, sharpen strategic understanding and bring greater attention to questions that will shape Bharat's national trajectory.

The fault lines may be changing. The responsibility to understand them remains ours.
